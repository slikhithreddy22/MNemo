Mnemo

A chest-worn camera memory assistant for people with short-term memory problems. It writes a timed text diary of what the wearer does with their hands, keeps no video, and answers questions such as “Did I close the fridge?” only from that diary, or says it didn’t see it.

Everything on this page is real output from the Kaggle runs (Qwen3-VL-4B on a T4 GPU) on EPIC-KITCHENS videos of five people our model never saw in training. Full design: Mnemo System Architecture.

How it works

Five layers, three of them ours

CaptureChest camera, 10 frames/s. Frames stay in RAM.standard
TriggerOur HOI-TCN model scores each 4 s for hand–object interaction; quiet windows are skipped.ours
DescribeQwen3-VL-4B writes what happened, in short lines.pre-trained
MemoryTimed diary plus open/closed, on/off states that re-check themselves.ours
AnswerOnly from diary lines, with the time, or “I didn’t see that”.ours

Measured results

What the numbers show

  • Matching is strict: a log line counts only with the same verb and object class as the EPIC annotation (“package” is not “box”), so recall reads low (26–31%).
  • The trigger threshold 0.6 was chosen on these same clips; a confirmation run on unseen clips is prepared.
  • The morning test has 38 questions, so its counts are small.
Data: EPIC-KITCHENS-100 (University of Bristol), test people P02, P06, P08, P14, P26. Models: Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, MediaPipe Hands, YOLOv8n, and our HOI-TCN (240,252 parameters). Run of 16 October 2026 on a Kaggle T4. Stored: text only.
